# Presentation design rationale

Countable's landing page starts with a question anyone can understand: how many new homes are ready for people to use? It explains why permits and plans cannot simply be added, what separate move-in proof establishes, and what the saved "receipt" would contain. The first screen shows four real Bedford records with overlapping unit claims and an unresolved result. The visual uses warm paper, dark ink, teal for source connections, and amber for missing evidence.

The page follows one **real source trail** throughout: Bedford's development application describes 70 units across eight buildings, A3's building permit describes nine apartments, its sprinkler permit repeats those nine and references the building permit, and a temporary-use application marked Completed says work under the building permit was pending. The 70 + 9 + 9 = 88 sum is shown only as a wrong way to add overlapping claims. These records cannot establish an occupancy date or a verified addition. The selected snapshot includes only four of the eight child-building permits and no occupancy document.

Major beats: the question; why Bedford's paperwork can mislead; how the app groups forms and shows the original words; why a person must check move-in approval; what remains unknown; documented demand for better housing data; Pittsburgh's reporting deadline; the temporary-use status trap; then a direct app handoff. The demand section cites the City Controller's reconciliation work and dashboard recommendation alongside WPRDC's dataset caveats. It explicitly avoids claiming City adoption or paying customers. A visitor can open `/app` at any point. The optional Present control advances sections without changing normal scrolling.

The app retains its record, evidence, decision, audit, and export flows. Larger text, spacing, and a short dismissible guide support a judge at a laptop or projected screen. The hackathon's public data catalog points to OneStopPGH for current case documents; the pitch treats it as a search lead, never as proof that Bedford's occupancy document is available there. No verified additions are claimed from the permit snapshot alone.

Research principles: Devpost's [demo guidance](https://help.devpost.com/article/84-video-making-best-practices) favors explaining the app immediately and showing the working product; [YC's pitch guidance](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-pitch-your-company/) favors a plain statement of what the product does; [Sequoia's framework](https://sequoiacap.com/article/writing-a-business-plan) informs the compressed problem–solution–why-now sequence. The graphic-led clarity of [Vercel Observability](https://vercel.com/products/observability), spacious interface storytelling of [Linear Plan](https://linear.app/plan), and direct hierarchy of [Stripe Radar](https://stripe.com/radar) informed the principles, without copying their assets or layouts.
