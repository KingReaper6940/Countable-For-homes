# Presentation design rationale

Countable's landing page starts with a question anyone can understand: how many new homes are ready for people to use? It explains why permits and plans cannot simply be added, what separate move-in proof establishes, and what the saved "receipt" contains. The first screen shows forms for one invented project leading to eight supported homes while another building still needs proof. The visual uses warm paper, dark ink, teal for connections, amber for pending evidence, and green only for a reviewed result.

The page follows one **illustrative scenario** throughout: a parent filing describes 24 planned units; building A describes 8; its sprinkler filing repeats the same 8; building B describes 16. In this invented walkthrough, an occupancy passage and human review support an 8-unit event for A, while B's 16 remain pending. The initial 24 + 8 + 8 + 16 = 56 sum is shown only as a wrong way to add overlapping claims. The number 24 is a planned project total, not a delivered count. The scenario does not enter the real ledger.

Major beats: the question; why paperwork can mislead; how the app groups forms and shows the original words; why a person must check move-in approval; what is supported versus still unknown; documented demand for better housing data; Pittsburgh's reporting deadline; then real records and a direct app handoff. The demand section cites the City Controller's reconciliation work and dashboard recommendation alongside WPRDC's dataset caveats. It explicitly avoids claiming City adoption or paying customers. A visitor can open `/app` at any point. The optional Present control advances sections without changing normal scrolling.

The app retains its record, evidence, decision, audit, and export flows. Larger text, spacing, and a short dismissible guide support a judge at a laptop or projected screen. Real public data receives a visually distinct section and an explicit coverage note. No verified real additions are claimed from the permit snapshot alone.

Research principles: Devpost's [demo guidance](https://help.devpost.com/article/84-video-making-best-practices) favors explaining the app immediately and showing the working product; [YC's pitch guidance](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-pitch-your-company/) favors a plain statement of what the product does; [Sequoia's framework](https://sequoiacap.com/article/writing-a-business-plan) informs the compressed problem–solution–why-now sequence. The graphic-led clarity of [Vercel Observability](https://vercel.com/products/observability), spacious interface storytelling of [Linear Plan](https://linear.app/plan), and direct hierarchy of [Stripe Radar](https://stripe.com/radar) informed the principles, without copying their assets or layouts.
