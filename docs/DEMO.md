# Five-minute demo

1. Open the review queue and note the WPRDC snapshot date and limited coverage.
2. Open the Bedford development. Compare the parent application's 70-unit source claim with separate building permits for A3, A4, A7, and A8. Do not add parent and child counts or call the child subset the full project.
3. Select the A3 building permit and sprinkler record. Their repeated nine-unit description is supporting evidence for one building, not two additions.
4. Compare A3 and A4: a shared parcel does not merge distinct buildings. Inspect the temporary-use record and the unresolved occupancy conditions.
5. Open the South Side conversion. Distinguish a proposal to add one dwelling from a resulting two-unit building. `Completed` remains a permit status, not an occupancy event date.
6. Switch into the clearly labeled synthetic sandbox to rehearse document review. Read the example occupancy text, cite its exact sentence supporting nine units and the event date, complete the six checks, and approve. Inspect the synthetic ledger and audit history. Refresh to show persistence, then reverse the decision and inspect the new audit entry.
7. Export CSV and the evidence report. Return to the real ledger to show that synthetic work did not enter it.

The example is an analyst workflow demonstration. The real snapshot alone cannot establish a verified occupancy addition.
